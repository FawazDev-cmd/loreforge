## 1. Company Overview

Acme Technologies builds secure cloud platforms for operational teams that manage sensitive business workflows. The company serves enterprise customers in logistics, financial operations, and regulated internal services. Our products are designed to be dependable, auditable, and understandable by both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

Every employee is expected to protect customer trust. That includes following security controls, documenting decisions, escalating operational risk, and treating internal information as company property. This handbook summarizes workplace expectations. Security-specific controls are defined in the Acme Technologies Information Security Policy.

## 2. Mission

Acme Technologies helps organizations turn complex operational knowledge into reliable action. We build software that makes teams faster without hiding the reasoning behind important decisions.

Our mission is supported by three operating principles:

- Make systems explainable.
- Protect customer and employee data.
- Improve reliability through disciplined engineering.

## 3. Core Values

### 3.1 Customer Trust

Customer trust is the default priority when speed and caution conflict. Employees must raise concerns when a decision could affect confidentiality, availability, data integrity, or contractual commitments.

### 3.2 Clear Ownership

Every project, service, and incident must have a named owner. Ownership means defining scope, communicating status, and driving the next action until the work is closed or handed off.

### 3.3 Written Decisions

Teams should record significant product, security, and operational decisions in durable documents. Engineering decisions that affect production systems should follow the review expectations in the Acme Technologies Engineering Handbook.

### 3.4 Respectful Collaboration

Acme Technologies expects direct, respectful communication. Disagreement is welcome when it improves outcomes. Harassment, intimidation, retaliation, or exclusionary conduct is not tolerated.

## 4. Working Hours

Standard business coverage is Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. local time for the employee's primary work location. Teams may define additional overlap hours when cross-region collaboration requires it.

Employees should keep calendars current and make availability visible. Meetings should include an agenda, an owner, and a decision or next step. Recurring meetings should be reviewed quarterly and removed when they no longer create value.

On-call schedules are handled separately from ordinary working hours. Production on-call expectations are defined in the Acme Technologies Incident Response Playbook.

## 5. Remote Work

Employees may work remotely up to three days per week with manager approval. Teams may approve additional remote days for focus work, health needs, travel constraints, or distributed project coverage.

Remote work requires:

- Reliable internet access.
- A private work area for confidential conversations.
- Use of company-managed devices for company work.
- MFA-protected access to company systems.
- Compliance with the device security requirements in the Information Security Policy.

Employees must not print confidential customer information at home unless approved by Legal and Security. Any suspected exposure of company data during remote work must be reported immediately through the incident reporting process.

## 6. Leave Policy

Acme Technologies provides paid time off for vacation, illness, personal obligations, bereavement, parental leave, jury duty, and company holidays. Employees should request planned leave at least two weeks in advance when practical.

Managers should approve leave unless it creates a documented business conflict that cannot be mitigated. Teams must maintain handoff notes for active projects, production responsibilities, and customer commitments before extended leave.

Unplanned sick leave should be reported to the manager as soon as reasonably possible. Employees are not expected to disclose private medical details.

## 7. Password Policy

Employees must follow the password and MFA requirements in the Acme Technologies Information Security Policy. At a minimum:

- Passwords must be at least 14 characters.
- Passwords must not be reused across personal and company systems.
- Password managers approved by Security must be used for company credentials.
- MFA is required for email, source control, cloud consoles, production tools, and administrative systems.
- Shared passwords are prohibited except through approved vault mechanisms.

Password rotation is required every 180 days for standard employee credentials and every 90 days for privileged or production administrator credentials. Immediate rotation is required after suspected compromise, role transfer, or vendor access removal.

Employees must never send passwords through chat, email, tickets, code comments, screenshots, or documents.

## 8. Acceptable Use

Company systems are provided for business purposes. Limited personal use is allowed when it does not interfere with work, create security risk, consume excessive resources, or violate law or policy.

Employees must not:

- Store customer data in personal accounts.
- Connect unapproved storage, synchronization, or AI tools to company repositories or documents.
- Disable endpoint protection, disk encryption, or mobile device management.
- Use company systems for harassment, unlawful activity, or unauthorized monitoring.
- Circumvent access controls or audit logging.

Security exceptions must be approved by Security and documented with an expiration date.

## 9. Code of Conduct

Employees must maintain a workplace that is professional, inclusive, and safe. The following behavior is expected:

- Treat colleagues, customers, candidates, and vendors with respect.
- Give feedback about work and impact, not personal attributes.
- Escalate conflict early when it affects delivery or psychological safety.
- Protect confidential information during meetings, demos, and public communication.
- Follow the incident communication rules in the Incident Response Playbook during operational events.

Policy violations may lead to coaching, loss of access, disciplinary action, or termination depending on severity.
